Why Do You Need an Interest & Loan Calculator?

Finance may look complicated at first glance, but at its core, it comes down to three main elements: principal amount, interest rate, and time. Whether you are depositing money into a savings account, investing in a scheme, or borrowing from a bank, interest is always involved.

Simple Interest Calculator

Simple interest is the most basic form of interest. It is calculated using the formula:

SI = (Principal × Rate × Time) / 100

For example, if you deposit ₹10,000 at 5% annual interest for 3 years, you'll earn ₹1,500 as interest.

Perfect for:

  • Short-term deposits and savings accounts
  • Educational projects and learning
  • Quick financial calculations
  • Understanding basic interest concepts

Compound Interest Calculator

Compound interest is often called the eighth wonder of the world because it makes money grow exponentially. Unlike simple interest, compound interest adds earned interest back into the principal.

A = P(1 + r/n)^(nt)

Where:

  • A = Total amount
  • P = Principal
  • r = Interest rate (decimal)
  • n = Compounding frequency per year
  • t = Time in years

Features:

  • Multiple compounding frequencies (Daily to Annually)
  • Regular contribution options (like monthly SIPs)
  • See how contributions accelerate growth
  • Ideal for long-term investment planning

Loan Calculator

Understanding monthly payments (EMIs) and total repayment cost is crucial before borrowing. The tool uses the standard EMI formula:

EMI = [P × r × (1+r)^n] / [(1+r)^n – 1]

Features:

  • Calculate EMI for home loans, car loans, personal loans
  • Choose payment frequency (Monthly, Bi-Weekly, Weekly)
  • View detailed amortization schedule
  • See breakdown of principal vs interest for each payment
  • Understand total interest paid over loan term

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between simple and compound interest?

Simple interest is calculated only on the principal amount throughout the investment period. The formula is: SI = (P × R × T) / 100.

Compound interest includes both the principal and accumulated interest from previous periods, leading to exponential growth. It's calculated using: A = P(1 + r/n)^(nt).

For example, ₹10,000 at 10% for 5 years:

  • Simple Interest: ₹5,000 interest (Total: ₹15,000)
  • Compound Interest (Annual): ₹6,105 interest (Total: ₹16,105)

What is an amortization schedule?

An amortization schedule is a detailed table that shows the breakdown of each loan payment into:

  • Payment Number: Sequential payment periods
  • Payment Amount: Total EMI paid
  • Interest Portion: Amount going toward interest
  • Principal Portion: Amount reducing the loan balance
  • Remaining Balance: Outstanding loan amount

This helps you understand how your loan gets paid off over time. Early payments have more interest, while later payments have more principal.
